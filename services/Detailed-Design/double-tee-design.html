Precast Double Tee Slab Design

We design precast prestressed double tee (DT) slabs for long-span floors and roofs in offices, car parks, industrial and public buildings. Each unit is checked from strand release in the factory, through demoulding, lifting and erection, to the final composite condition with the structural topping in place. A recent design covered 1800 mm wide, 830 mm deep double tees spanning 15.5 m, with a 75 mm structural topping, 16 low-relaxation 12.7 mm strands and dapped ends seated on cast-in-situ beams.

The design checks we perform

Prestress losses and transfer stresses

We calculate initial and long-term losses (PCI simplified method) and check top and bottom fibre stresses at release, including the concrete strength required for transfer and first lift.

Service stresses and composite action

We check the unshored unit under its own weight and the wet topping, then the composite section under superimposed dead and live load, against the ACI 318-19 stress limits.

Ultimate flexural capacity

We confirm the design moment strength along the full span, including strand development near the ends. For the 15.5 m unit, the factored moment of 1,267 kN·m is resisted by a design strength of 1,755 kN·m.

Vertical and horizontal shear

We design the stem shear reinforcement for the critical sections near the supports, and check horizontal (interface) shear between the precast flange and the topping.

Camber, deflection and vibration

We track camber and deflection at transfer, erection, completion and final service using PCI multipliers, and check the floor's fundamental frequency against walking-vibration limits (5.8 Hz against a 3.0 Hz minimum).

Dapped-end connections and bearing

We design the dapped ends to the PCI Design Handbook: the depth of the cut, the shear span-to-depth ratio, direct shear, hanger and flexural-tension reinforcement, with a horizontal restraint force of 20% of the vertical reaction. We also confirm the bearing length (150 mm provided against 86 mm required for span/180).

Demoulding, lifting and handling

We size the lifting anchors with factors for mould adhesion, crane dynamics and sling angle, and check the anchor-bar development length and stresses in the unit at each lifting stage.

Governing standards and how we apply them

ACI 318-19

Primary code for load combinations, prestressed flexure and shear strength, serviceability stress limits, bearing length, and anchorage of lifting inserts.

PCI Design Handbook (MNL-120, 7th edition)

Applied for prestress losses, camber and deflection multipliers, dapped-end design and handling of double tee units.

ASTM A416 and BS 4449

Grade 270 (1860 MPa) low-relaxation seven-wire strand and Grade 500B reinforcement, with C48/60 precast concrete and a C32/40 topping.

Why choose Precasterz for double tee design

Factory-to-floor verification

We check every stage the unit goes through, because transfer, demoulding and lifting often govern a long, slender double tee before the final load does.

Connection detailing that can be built

Our dapped-end and bearing details are drawn with real covers, clearances and bar bends, so the reinforcement fits in the stem and the unit seats correctly on site.

Complete calculation package

You receive a structured design memo with criteria, loading plans, analysis output, connection and lifting checks, and the final reinforcement details, ready for consultant approval.
